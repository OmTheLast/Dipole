How to read the trends and uncertainty

Each trend is the slope of a straight line through annual temperatures relative to the 1951–1980 average. For the global comparison we fit the yearly local minus global differences. A negative difference means a place warmed more slowly than the world or cooled more quickly. The chart shows the two original histories.

In the yearly view, each cell needs all 12 months in the selected year. For a trend it needs all 12 months in every year of the period. Gray marks missing estimates. Regional averages weight cells by area and require at least 80% coverage each month. If a region has gaps, its fitted line uses the uninterrupted record after the last gap. Its global comparison uses exactly those years.

The 95% interval describes the fitted trend for the selected place. We account for similarities between nearby years using Newey–West standard errors with three annual lags. The interval excludes additional uncertainty in NASA’s temperature estimates. Map colors show estimated rates. Examining many locations can produce chance findings.
